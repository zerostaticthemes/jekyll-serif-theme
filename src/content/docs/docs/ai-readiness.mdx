---
title: AI readiness and use-case discovery
description: A practical framework for evaluating AI opportunities, data fit, risks, and evaluation needs.
---

An AI initiative is easier to evaluate when it starts with a concrete task rather than a model or tool. Describe who needs help, what work they do today, and what a useful result would look like before choosing an implementation.

## Frame the use case

Write down the decision or task to improve, the people affected, and the current process. Identify where an AI-assisted step could fit and where a person should remain responsible for review or approval.

Prefer a measurable question over a broad ambition. For example, define whether a system should find relevant passages in an approved knowledge collection, classify incoming requests for review, or draft a response for a person to edit.

## Check data and access

List the information the workflow would need, who owns it, where it comes from, and whether it is complete and current enough for the task. Check access permissions, retention expectations, sensitive fields, and whether the proposed use is appropriate for the information.

For knowledge-assistance patterns, establish which sources are authoritative and how updates, access controls, and citations will be handled. Retrieval can make relevant material available to a model, but it does not by itself make that material accurate or complete.

## Define risks and evaluation

Consider incorrect or incomplete outputs, inappropriate disclosure, harmful or biased responses, prompt manipulation, and changes in the source data. Decide which cases need escalation, what the system must refuse, and how users can report a problem.

Build an evaluation set from representative tasks, including difficult and out-of-scope examples. Assess output quality against clear criteria, test failure cases, and involve the people who will use or oversee the workflow. Define how performance and incidents will be monitored after release.

## Start with a bounded experiment

Choose a limited workflow, a representative data sample, and a named owner. Agree on success measures, review responsibilities, and stop conditions before a pilot begins. Expand only when the evidence supports the next step and the operating responsibilities are clear.
